# K2 gates: retrieval modes, analyzers, lexical backends, cited answers

Measured 2026-10-04 with `scripts/bench_k2.py` (raw numbers: `k2.json`; the 30 live answers:
`k2_answers.jsonl`), operonx `feat/kb-upstream`, on a shared 24-core CPU machine (torch pinned
to 8 threads; other services were running, so latencies are indicative). Each set is ingested
through the real ingest graph into its own collection; every number comes from an operonx
`Eval` run of the search flow with the KB's evaluators (PLAN R8).

**Models (PLAN R10).** Embedder `intfloat/multilingual-e5-small` (MIT, 118M parameters,
384 dims, revision `614241f`): trained for query–passage retrieval on multilingual data that
includes Vietnamese, mean-pooled exactly as operonx's `HFEmbedding` pools, small enough for CPU;
its `query: `/`passage: ` prefixes come from `DenseIndexSpec` templates. BGE-M3 was not used: it
is CLS-pooled, which `HFEmbedding` does not do, and is 5x larger. Reranker
`cross-encoder/mmarco-mMiniLMv2-L12-H384-v1` (Apache-2.0, multilingual, revision `1427fd6`)
through operonx's `HFReranker`, reranking the top 30 hybrid hits: `BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3`
took 10.5 s per 30 pairs on this CPU against 1.6 s, too slow to evaluate 914 queries.

**Sets (PLAN R9).**

| `xquad_vi` | XQuAD vi, google-deepmind/xquad @ `7d30520`, SHA-256 `f619a1eb…`, CC BY-SA 4.0 | 48 | 170 | 397 |

XQuAD cases are every third question; the label is the sentence holding the answer's start.
`corpus_vi` cases are one per fact sentence of the 40 Vietnamese documents (md, html, txt, docx,

## Gate: hybrid becomes the default

Hybrid beats dense on Recall@10 on **3 of 3** sets (the gate asks for 2 of 3), so
`KnowledgeBase.search` uses hybrid for a collection with both indexes (`DEFAULT_MODE`), and the
only index a collection has otherwise.

| set | mode | R@5 | R@10 | R@20 | MRR | nDCG@10 | p50 ms | p95 ms | cases scored |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| xquad_vi | dense | 0.970 | **0.977** | 0.987 | 0.918 | 0.932 | 88 | 99 | 397/397 |
| xquad_vi | lexical | 0.980 | **0.998** | 0.998 | 0.943 | 0.956 | 26 | 30 | 397/397 |
| xquad_vi | hybrid | 0.995 | **0.998** | 0.998 | 0.960 | 0.969 | 93 | 107 | 397/397 |
| xquad_vi | hybrid+rerank | 0.985 | **0.990** | 1.000 | 0.953 | 0.962 | 4848 | 5418 | 397/397 |
| xquad_en | dense | 0.982 | **0.995** | 1.000 | 0.938 | 0.952 | 85 | 101 | 397/397 |
| xquad_en | lexical | 0.990 | **0.990** | 0.992 | 0.955 | 0.964 | 24 | 27 | 397/397 |
| xquad_en | hybrid | 0.992 | **0.998** | 1.000 | 0.967 | 0.975 | 88 | 98 | 397/397 |
| xquad_en | hybrid+rerank | 0.990 | **0.992** | 1.000 | 0.972 | 0.977 | 4612 | 5350 | 397/397 |
| corpus_vi | dense | 0.908 | **0.966** | 1.000 | 0.746 | 0.797 | 95 | 109 | 119/120 |

### hybrid − dense, Recall@10 (paired bootstrap, 2000 resamples)

| set | mean | 95% CI | cases better / worse |
|---|---|---|---|
| xquad_vi | +0.0202 | [+0.0050, +0.0378] | 9 / 1 |
| xquad_en | +0.0025 | [-0.0050, +0.0126] | 2 / 1 |
| corpus_vi | +0.0336 | [+0.0084, +0.0672] | 4 / 0 |


Reading the table:

- **The sets are near saturation at Recall@10** (a few hundred chunks each). The difference that
  decides the gate is significant on `xquad_vi` and `corpus_vi` and inside the noise on
  `xquad_en`; MRR and nDCG separate the modes more, and hybrid has the best MRR of the three first-stage modes on both XQuAD sets.
  A larger corpus (the internal one, D5) is what will make Recall@10 discriminate.
- **Lexical alone is strongest on `corpus_vi`**: its questions name the unit (`khối Tài chính
  miền Trung`) word for word, which is how templated synthetic questions are. Dense confuses
  units (MRR 0.75).
- **Rerank does not pay here**: the multilingual MiniLM cross-encoder lowers MRR on both
  Vietnamese sets, helps `xquad_en` by 0.005, and adds ~4.5 s per query on CPU. It stays
  opt-in (`reranker=`).
- Latency is the Eval item time (search flow end to end): lexical ~25 ms, dense ~90 ms (one
  query embedding on CPU), hybrid ~90-145 ms (both concurrently, plus fusion).

## Analyzer: `vi` + diacritic folding for Vietnamese

Lexical retrieval only, on each Vietnamese set, with the questions as written and with their
diacritics stripped (how people often type them):

| set | analyzer | R@10 accented | MRR accented | R@10 unaccented | MRR unaccented |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| xquad_vi | simple | 0.987 | 0.942 | 0.640 | 0.465 |
| xquad_vi | simple+fold | 0.960 | 0.886 | 0.960 | 0.886 |
| xquad_vi | vi | 0.995 | 0.947 | 0.645 | 0.477 |
| xquad_vi | vi+fold | 0.998 | 0.943 | 0.998 | 0.943 |
| corpus_vi | simple | 1.000 | 0.809 | 0.639 | 0.316 |
| corpus_vi | simple+fold | 0.992 | 0.759 | 0.992 | 0.759 |
| corpus_vi | vi | 1.000 | 0.859 | 0.639 | 0.318 |
| corpus_vi | vi+fold | 1.000 | 0.864 | 1.000 | 0.864 |


Folding is not free: alone (`simple+fold`) it costs 0.03 Recall@10 and 0.05 MRR on accented
questions, because words that differ only by their marks merge. The syllable bigrams of `vi` win
that back (a folded bigram is far less ambiguous than a folded syllable), so `vi+fold` matches
the best accented result and takes unaccented questions from 0.64 to 1.00 Recall@10. Without
folding an unaccented question loses a third of its answers. The gate ran with `vi+fold` on the
Vietnamese sets and `simple` on English. The `AnalyzerSpec` default stays `simple` (English and
others were not measured against `vi`); a Vietnamese collection should set
`AnalyzerSpec(kind="vi", fold_diacritics=True)`.

## Lexical backends: SQLite FTS5 (bm25) against Postgres FTS (ts_rank_cd)

Same analyzer, same tokens:

| set | backend | R@10 | MRR | nDCG@10 | p50 ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| xquad_vi | SQLite FTS5 bm25 | 0.998 | 0.943 | 0.956 | 26 |
| xquad_vi | Postgres ts_rank_cd | 0.909 | 0.678 | 0.733 | 60 |
| xquad_en | SQLite FTS5 bm25 | 0.990 | 0.955 | 0.964 | 24 |
| xquad_en | Postgres ts_rank_cd | 0.600 | 0.330 | 0.387 | 34 |
| corpus_vi | SQLite FTS5 bm25 | 1.000 | 0.864 | 0.898 | 31 |
| corpus_vi | Postgres ts_rank_cd | 0.698 | 0.490 | 0.535 | 89 |


`ts_rank_cd` has no inverse document frequency, so an OR of the query's words ranks chunks
full of common words (`the`, `of`, `của`) as high as the rare word that matters: Recall@10 drops
by 0.09 to 0.39. Postgres FTS is correct (it passes the same filter conformance suite) but is a
weak ranker for natural-language queries; a Postgres deployment that needs lexical quality
wants BM25 (ParadeDB, track5 §9.3) or the SQLite index. Recorded, not fixed in K2.

## Cited answers (live check)

`gpt-4o-mini` (from Operon's `resources.yaml`; no key printed), 10 random cases per set,
answering from the hybrid search's top 6 hits (`k2_answers.jsonl`):

| | xquad_vi | xquad_en | corpus_vi | all |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| citations verified / given | 9 / 10 | 7 / 10 | 9 / 10 | 25 / 30 (0.83) |
| faithfulness proxy (Eval run) | 0.64 | 0.41 | 0.78 | |
| grounded recall (Eval run) | 0.70 | 0.50 | 0.70 | |

Each of the 5 dropped citations was checked against the canonical text, and each is the model
not quoting verbatim, not the verifier failing:

- the first letter lowercased (twice: Perotti in English, Thoreau in Vietnamese);
- a sentence cut mid-way and closed with a full stop (Thoreau in English: the source goes on
  ", and later by Gandhi");
- the source's double quotes turned into single quotes (`"design build"` → `'design build'`),
  most likely to avoid escaping inside the JSON string;
- a sentence that is in another source cited under source [1] (`cvi-115`: the sentence is in
  `doc_115.txt`, not in the cited source).

All were dropped and reported and their sentences listed as unsupported; none was shown.

**Gate status: citation precision ≥ 0.9 is not met** (0.83 on 30 live answers), and the PLAN's
human verification of 30 samples is still to be done (`k2_answers.jsonl` is that sample). The
verifier's strictness is by design (R6). Two of the five failures (letter case at the start, `"`
written as `'`) would pass a verifier that ignores the case of the first letter and the kind of
quotation mark; whether to accept that is a design decision left open, to be measured on the
human-checked sample before it is changed.
